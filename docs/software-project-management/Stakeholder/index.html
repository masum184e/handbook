Stakeholder

A stakeholder in Software Project Management (SPM) is any individual, group, or organization that can influence, is influenced by, or has an interest in the software project and its outcomes. Stakeholders may participate directly in the project, provide resources, make decisions, use the software, or be affected by its success or failure.

Stakeholders are important because they define project requirements, provide feedback, approve deliverables, allocate resources, and ensure the project aligns with business goals.

Simple Definition: A stakeholder is any person, group, or organization that has an interest in or is affected by a software project.

Objectives of Stakeholder Management

The main objectives of stakeholder management are to:

  • Identify all project stakeholders.
  • Understand their needs and expectations.
  • Maintain effective communication.
  • Gain stakeholder support and commitment.
  • Resolve conflicts among stakeholders.
  • Ensure project success by satisfying stakeholder requirements.

Importance of Stakeholders in Software Projects

Stakeholders play a vital role throughout the software development lifecycle.

Importance includes:​

  • Define project goals and requirements.
  • Provide funding and resources.
  • Approve project plans and deliverables.
  • Give feedback during development.
  • Help identify project risks.
  • Ensure the software meets business and user needs.
  • Increase the likelihood of project success.

Without stakeholder involvement, software projects may fail due to unclear requirements, poor communication, or lack of user acceptance.

Types of Stakeholders

Stakeholders are generally classified into Internal Stakeholders and External Stakeholders.

1. Internal Stakeholders​

Internal stakeholders belong to the organization developing the software.

Examples​

  • Project Manager
  • Software Developers
  • Quality Assurance (QA) Engineers
  • Business Analysts
  • UI/UX Designers
  • Database Administrators
  • System Administrators
  • Senior Management

Explanation​

Internal stakeholders are directly involved in planning, developing, testing, and managing the project.

2. External Stakeholders​

External stakeholders are outside the development organization but have an interest in the project.

Examples​

  • Clients
  • Customers
  • End Users
  • Suppliers
  • Government Agencies
  • Investors
  • Business Partners

Explanation​

External stakeholders influence project requirements, funding, legal compliance, and software acceptance.

Major Stakeholders and Their Roles

1. Project Sponsor​

The project sponsor provides financial support and overall project direction.

Responsibilities​

  • Approves project budget.
  • Provides resources.
  • Makes high-level decisions.
  • Reviews project progress.

Example​

A bank funds the development of a mobile banking application.

2. Project Manager​

The project manager plans, executes, monitors, and controls the software project.

Responsibilities​

  • Prepare project plans.
  • Manage schedule and budget.
  • Allocate resources.
  • Manage risks.
  • Communicate with stakeholders.

Example​

The project manager coordinates developers, testers, and clients to ensure timely delivery.

3. Client​

The client requests the software and defines business requirements.

Responsibilities​

  • Provide project requirements.
  • Approve deliverables.
  • Review project progress.
  • Accept the final software.

Example​

A hospital hires a software company to build a Hospital Management System.

4. End Users​

End users are the people who actually use the software.

Responsibilities​

  • Provide feedback.
  • Participate in user acceptance testing (UAT).
  • Suggest improvements.

Example​

Doctors and nurses use a Hospital Management System daily.

5. Software Developers​

Developers build the software according to specifications.

Responsibilities​

  • Coding
  • Debugging
  • Integration
  • Documentation

Example​

Frontend developers create the user interface, while backend developers implement business logic.

6. Quality Assurance (QA) Team​

The QA team ensures software quality.

Responsibilities​

  • Test software.
  • Identify defects.
  • Verify bug fixes.
  • Ensure quality standards.

Example​

QA engineers perform functional, integration, and performance testing before release.

7. Business Analyst​

The business analyst bridges the gap between clients and developers.

Responsibilities​

  • Gather requirements.
  • Analyze business processes.
  • Prepare requirement documents.
  • Clarify stakeholder expectations.

Example​

The business analyst interviews users and prepares the Software Requirements Specification (SRS).

8. Senior Management​

Senior management provides organizational support.

Responsibilities​

  • Allocate budgets.
  • Approve strategic decisions.
  • Monitor organizational goals.

Example​

The company's CEO approves funding for a new AI-based software product.

Stakeholder Classification

Stakeholders can also be classified based on their power and interest.

High Power, Low InterestCan influence the project but are less involved in daily work.Keep satisfied with regular updates.
Low Power, High InterestInterested in the project but have limited decision-making authority.Keep informed and gather feedback.
Low Power, Low InterestMinimal influence and limited interest.Monitor with occasional communication.

Stakeholder Communication

Effective communication is essential for project success.

Communication Methods​

  • Meetings
  • Emails
  • Progress reports
  • Video conferences
  • Dashboards
  • Demonstrations
  • Status presentations

Benefits​

  • Reduces misunderstandings.
  • Builds trust.
  • Improves collaboration.
  • Resolves issues quickly.
  • Keeps stakeholders informed.

Stakeholder Management Process

Step 1: Identify Stakeholders​

Prepare a list of everyone involved or affected.

Example​

For an Online Shopping System:

  • Client
  • Customers
  • Developers
  • Testers
  • Project Manager
  • Payment Gateway Provider

Step 2: Analyze Stakeholders​

Understand:

  • Their interests.
  • Their influence.
  • Their expectations.
  • Their communication needs.

Step 3: Plan Stakeholder Engagement​

Decide:

  • How often to communicate.
  • What information to share.
  • Which communication methods to use.

Step 4: Engage Stakeholders​

Keep stakeholders involved through:

  • Review meetings.
  • Demonstrations.
  • Progress reports.
  • Feedback sessions.

Step 5: Monitor Stakeholder Satisfaction​

Collect feedback and resolve concerns throughout the project.

Example: Stakeholders in an Online Shopping System

Suppose a software company is developing an Online Shopping System.

StakeholderRoleContribution
ClientRequests the systemDefines business requirements and approves the final product.
Project ManagerManages the projectPlans schedules, budgets, resources, and risks.
Business AnalystRequirement gatheringDocuments business needs and translates them into technical requirements.
DevelopersBuild the softwareDevelop frontend, backend, and database components.
QA EngineersTest the softwareEnsure the application works correctly and meets quality standards.
UI/UX DesignerDesigns interfacesCreates user-friendly and visually appealing screens.
CustomersEnd usersPurchase products and provide feedback on usability.
Payment Gateway ProviderExternal serviceProcesses secure online payments.

Explanation​

Each stakeholder contributes differently:

  • The client defines what features the system should include.
  • The business analyst converts business needs into detailed requirements.
  • Developers implement the features.
  • QA engineers test the system for bugs and performance.
  • Customers use the application and provide feedback that helps improve future versions.
  • The payment gateway provider ensures secure and reliable payment processing.

Because all stakeholders have different roles and expectations, effective communication and coordination are essential for project success.

Real-World Example: Hospital Management System

A software company develops a Hospital Management System.

Stakeholders​

StakeholderRole
Hospital AdministrationProject sponsor and client
DoctorsEnd users
NursesEnd users
PatientsIndirect beneficiaries
Project ManagerProject planning and execution
DevelopersSoftware development
QA TeamSoftware testing
Database AdministratorDatabase management
Government Health AuthorityEnsures legal and regulatory compliance

Explanation​

  • The hospital administration provides funding and approves project milestones.
  • Doctors and nurses use the system to manage patient records and appointments, so their feedback is essential for usability.
  • The government health authority ensures that the system complies with healthcare regulations and data privacy laws.
  • The development team and QA team collaborate to build and test a secure, reliable system that meets stakeholder expectations.

Advantages of Effective Stakeholder Management

  • Clear project requirements.
  • Better communication.
  • Improved decision-making.
  • Higher customer satisfaction.
  • Reduced project risks.
  • Faster conflict resolution.
  • Increased stakeholder trust.
  • Greater project success rate.

Challenges in Stakeholder Management

  • Conflicting stakeholder expectations.
  • Frequent requirement changes.
  • Poor communication.
  • Resistance to change.
  • Limited stakeholder availability.
  • Differing priorities among stakeholders.

Best Practices

  • Identify stakeholders early in the project.
  • Understand each stakeholder's needs and expectations.
  • Develop a stakeholder communication plan.
  • Keep stakeholders informed with regular updates.
  • Encourage active participation during reviews and testing.
  • Address conflicts promptly and transparently.
  • Document decisions and agreed requirements.
  • Continuously monitor stakeholder satisfaction.
