What a Band 7 Candidate Generally Needs

For someone targeting Band 7, you should be able to:

Fluency​

Speak at reasonable length without frequent breakdowns.

Vocabulary​

Use a reasonably wide range of vocabulary and paraphrase when necessary.

Grammar​

Use both simple and complex structures with generally good control.

Pronunciation​

Be generally easy to understand, even if you have an accent.

A common mistake is thinking:

"I need perfect English for Band 7."

You don't.

IELTS recognizes that even high-band candidates can make occasional mistakes.

Part 1 vs Part 2 vs Part 3

A useful way to think about the three sections is:

Part 1​

Personal → familiar

"What do you do in your free time?"

Part 2​

Personal experience → extended speech

"Describe an activity you enjoy."

Part 3​

General/abstract → analysis

"Why do people have less free time nowadays?"

So the difficulty generally progresses:

Personal → Extended → Analytical

Should You Memorize Answers?

I strongly recommend not memorizing complete answers.

Instead, prepare:

  • Useful vocabulary
  • Common topics
  • Personal examples
  • Ideas
  • Sentence structures
  • Linking expressions

For example, learn flexible expressions such as:

One of the main reasons is...

Another factor is...

From my perspective...

For example...

Having said that...

Compared with the past...

This is particularly important because...

Then adapt them to the question.

What Happens If You Don't Understand the Question?

You can ask the examiner to repeat it.

For example:

Sorry, could you repeat the question, please?

Or:

danger

Could you explain what you mean by that?

This is much better than answering a completely different question.

What If You Make a Mistake?

Don't panic.

Simply correct yourself and continue.

For example:

I went there last year—sorry, I mean two years ago—and I really enjoyed the experience.

One mistake does not destroy your score.

The examiner evaluates your overall performance.

What If You Have No Idea About the Topic?

You don't need expert knowledge.

IELTS Speaking is primarily testing your English communication, not your knowledge of the world.

You can say:

That's an interesting question. I've never really thought about it before, but I'd say...

Then develop a reasonable answer.

For example:

I've never really thought about that before, but I'd say people probably spend less time talking to their neighbors nowadays because they have busier lifestyles and can communicate with friends online.

You don't need specialist knowledge.

Common Mistakes

❌ Giving extremely short answers​

Examiner: Do you like music?

Candidate: Yes.

This doesn't give you enough opportunity to demonstrate your English.

❌ Giving memorized speeches​

If the examiner asks something slightly different, a memorized answer can become unnatural and difficult to adapt.

❌ Speaking too quickly​

Fast speech doesn't automatically mean fluent speech.

❌ Using difficult vocabulary unnecessarily​

Accuracy and natural usage are more important.

❌ Repeating the same words​

Instead of repeatedly saying:

good, good, good...

you can use:

useful enjoyable beneficial effective interesting valuable

—but only when they genuinely fit the context.

❌ Ignoring the question​

Make sure your answer directly addresses what the examiner asked.

A Practical Answer Formula​

For many questions, you can use:

Part 1​

Answer → Reason → Example

Yes, I enjoy cooking because it helps me relax. For example, I usually cook something simple on weekends when I have more free time.

Part 2​

Introduction → Background → Details → Example → Feelings/importance → Conclusion

Part 3​

Opinion → Reason → Explanation → Example → Contrast/qualification

For example:

I think technology has made education more accessible. This is mainly because students can now access courses and educational materials from almost anywhere. For example, someone living in a rural area can watch lectures online without travelling to a major city. However, access to reliable internet is still a problem in some areas.

How You Should Prepare

A practical preparation routine could look like this:

Step 1 — Build topic vocabulary​

Prepare vocabulary for approximately:

  • Education
  • Technology
  • Environment
  • Health
  • Work
  • Hobbies
  • Travel
  • Food
  • Family
  • Society
  • Transportation
  • Media
  • Communication

Step 2 — Practice Part 1​

Take a topic and answer 5–10 questions aloud.

Don't write full answers.

Step 3 — Practice cue cards​

Take a random cue card.

Set a timer:

1 minute preparation → 2 minutes speaking

Record yourself.

Then listen and identify:

  • Long pauses
  • Repeated words
  • Grammar mistakes
  • Pronunciation problems
  • Weak vocabulary

Step 4 — Practice Part 3​

For every Part 2 topic, create broader questions.

For example:

Part 2: Describe a technology you use.

Then:

How has technology changed education?

Are young people too dependent on technology?

Will technology replace teachers?

What problems can technology create?

This trains you to move from personal experience to abstract discussion.

The Most Important Principle

Don't think:

"I need to sound like a native speaker."

Think:

"I need to communicate my ideas clearly, naturally, and accurately."

For IELTS Speaking, you want to demonstrate four things consistently:

Fluent communication + appropriate vocabulary + grammatical control + clear pronunciation.
