# 3. IELTS Reading

Reading lasts **60 minutes**.

There are:

**40 questions**

You normally have:

* 3 passages in Academic Reading
* 3 sections in General Training Reading

---

# Academic Reading

Academic passages may come from topics such as:

* science
* technology
* history
* psychology
* environment
* education
* biology
* economics

You don't necessarily need specialist knowledge.

The important skill is finding and interpreting information from the text.

---

# Major Reading Question Types

## 1. Multiple Choice

Choose the correct answer.

## 2. True / False / Not Given

You must distinguish between:

**TRUE**

The statement agrees with the passage.

**FALSE**

The statement contradicts the passage.

**NOT GIVEN**

The passage does not provide enough information.

This is one of the most important distinctions in IELTS Reading.

---

## 3. Yes / No / Not Given

Similar idea, but generally used for **opinions or claims**.

---

## 4. Matching Headings

You match headings to paragraphs.

This tests whether you understand the **main idea**.

---

## 5. Matching Information

You identify which paragraph contains particular information.

---

## 6. Matching Features

You match information with:

* people
* theories
* places
* dates
* discoveries

---

## 7. Sentence Completion

You complete sentences using words from the passage.

---

## 8. Summary Completion

You complete a summary.

---

## 9. Diagram Label Completion

You identify parts of a diagram.

---

# Reading Strategy for Band 8–9

Don't try to understand every word.

Instead:

### Step 1 — Read the question

Identify important keywords.

### Step 2 — Predict possible synonyms

For example:

Question:

> The project was extremely expensive.

Possible passage wording:

> The initiative required substantial financial investment.

"expensive" → "substantial financial investment"

### Step 3 — Scan the passage

Find the relevant area.

### Step 4 — Read around the answer

Don't simply match identical words.

IELTS frequently uses **paraphrasing**.

---

# Reading Band 8–9 Target

Approximately:

| Correct | Approx. Band |
| ------: | -----------: |
|   39–40 |            9 |
|   37–38 |          8.5 |
|   35–36 |            8 |
|   33–34 |          7.5 |
|   30–32 |            7 |

The exact conversion can vary between tests.

For Band 8, therefore, you should train toward **35–36+ correct answers consistently**.

# 4. IELTS Writing

Writing lasts:

**60 minutes**

There are two tasks.

### Task 1

### Task 2

Task 2 is worth **more** than Task 1 in the overall Writing score.

---

# IELTS Academic Writing Task 1

You are given a visual such as:

* line graph
* bar chart
* pie chart
* table
* process
* map
* combination of visuals

You normally write **at least 150 words**.

You should spend approximately:

**20 minutes**

on Task 1.

---

# Task 1 Structure

A strong structure is:

### Introduction

Paraphrase the question.

### Overview

Give the most important overall trends/features.

### Body Paragraph 1

Explain important details.

### Body Paragraph 2

Explain remaining important details/comparisons.

---

# Example

Suppose the graph shows smartphone ownership from 2010–2020.

Weak overview:

> Smartphone ownership increased.

Better:

> Overall, smartphone ownership increased substantially over the period, with the most pronounced growth occurring after 2015.

The second version demonstrates better:

* overview
* vocabulary
* precision
* grammatical control

---

# IELTS General Training Task 1

You normally write a **letter**.

It can be:

* formal
* semi-formal
* informal

For example:

> Write a letter to your landlord about a problem with your apartment.

---

# IELTS Writing Task 2

This is an **essay**.

You normally write **at least 250 words**.

A strong Band 8–9 response usually has:

### Introduction

* paraphrase topic
* clear position/thesis

### Body Paragraph 1

* main idea
* explanation
* example
* consequence/extension

### Body Paragraph 2

* main idea
* explanation
* example
* consequence/extension

### Conclusion

* summarize position
* don't introduce a completely new argument

---

# Your preferred structure can be:

## Introduction

**Background → Paraphrase → Thesis**

## Body

**Point → Explanation → Example → Result**

## Conclusion

**Summary → Final position**

This is particularly useful because it prevents your essay from becoming a collection of disconnected ideas.

---

# IELTS Writing Assessment

Writing is assessed using four criteria:

## 1. Task Response

Are you actually answering the question?

For Band 8–9:

* address all parts
* develop ideas
* explain ideas clearly
* provide relevant examples
* maintain a clear position

---

## 2. Coherence and Cohesion

This concerns how logically your writing is organized.

The examiner looks at:

* paragraphing
* progression
* linking
* referencing
* logical relationships

Don't simply use:

> Firstly, secondly, moreover, furthermore, in addition...

in every paragraph.

Natural cohesion is better.

---

# 3. Lexical Resource

This means vocabulary.

Band 8–9 vocabulary is **not about using complicated words everywhere**.

Compare:

> This problem is very big.

with:

> This issue has become increasingly significant.

But using unnecessarily complicated words can actually hurt clarity.

The goal is:

**precise + natural + appropriate vocabulary.**

---

# 4. Grammatical Range and Accuracy

You should demonstrate a mixture of:

* simple sentences
* compound sentences
* complex sentences
* relative clauses
* conditionals
* concessive clauses
* participle structures

For example:

> Although technology has transformed the way people communicate, excessive reliance on digital platforms can weaken face-to-face interaction.

This demonstrates more grammatical range than:

> Technology is useful. People use it every day. It helps communication.

But complexity must remain **accurate and natural**.

# Speaking

# Band 7 vs Band 8 vs Band 9

This distinction is extremely important.

## Band 7

Generally:

* good vocabulary
* generally clear organization
* some complex grammar
* some errors
* can discuss familiar and unfamiliar topics

## Band 8

Generally:

* wider and more precise vocabulary
* strong organization
* flexible grammar
* very good fluency
* errors are relatively infrequent
* ideas are developed effectively

## Band 9

Very high-level control:

* highly natural communication
* extremely flexible language
* sophisticated vocabulary used appropriately
* excellent grammatical control
* effortless organization
* pronunciation is consistently easy to understand

# A Strong IELTS Preparation Plan

If your target is **Band 8–9**, I would divide preparation into four stages.

## Stage 1 — Build English Foundation

Focus on:

* grammar
* vocabulary
* pronunciation
* reading
* listening

Don't immediately start doing full IELTS tests every day.

---

# Stage 2 — Learn IELTS Techniques

Learn:

### Listening

* predicting answers
* identifying keywords
* recognizing paraphrases
* handling distractors

### Reading

* skimming
* scanning
* keyword identification
* paraphrasing
* True/False/Not Given

### Writing

* essay structures
* argument development
* cohesion
* vocabulary
* grammar

### Speaking

* developing answers
* giving examples
* extending ideas
* expressing opinions
* comparing
* qualifying opinions

---

# Stage 3 — Intensive Practice

A possible weekly schedule:

| Day       | Main Focus                      |
| --------- | ------------------------------- |
| Saturday  | Listening + vocabulary          |
| Sunday    | Reading                         |
| Monday    | Writing Task 1                  |
| Tuesday   | Speaking Part 1 + 2             |
| Wednesday | Writing Task 2                  |
| Thursday  | Speaking Part 3                 |
| Friday    | Full mock test + error analysis |

The **error analysis** is extremely important.

Don't just record:

> Reading: 32/40

Instead record:

| Question | Error | Reason              |
| -------- | ----- | ------------------- |
| 5        | Wrong | Misread question    |
| 12       | Wrong | Vocabulary          |
| 19       | Wrong | Not Given confusion |
| 27       | Wrong | Time management     |

Then you know exactly what to improve.

---

# Stage 4 — Band 8–9 Optimization

At this stage, your focus changes.

You aren't simply learning English.

You are reducing:

* grammar errors
* pronunciation problems
* repetition
* weak vocabulary
* irrelevant ideas
* poor paragraph organization
* hesitation
* misunderstanding of questions

Your goal becomes **consistency**.

# The Biggest Mistakes Band 8 Candidates Make

### 2. Using unnecessarily complicated vocabulary

Sophisticated ≠ difficult.

### 3. Using linking words excessively

Every sentence doesn't need:

> Moreover, furthermore, however, consequently...

### 4. Writing without answering the exact question

Task Response is critical.

### 5. Giving undeveloped ideas

A claim without explanation isn't enough.

### 6. Ignoring pronunciation

Knowing vocabulary doesn't mean you can communicate it clearly.

### 7. Practicing without analyzing mistakes

Practice alone isn't enough.

**Practice → Analyze → Correct → Practice again**

is much more effective.
