listen

Basic Test Structure​

FeatureIELTS Listening
Questions per partUsually 10
Audio playedOnce only
Number of speakersVaries
Maximum raw score40

The four recordings become progressively more challenging.

Overall structure​

4 recordings → 40 questions → 1 mark per question → 40 marks total

The difficulty generally increases:

Part 1 → Part 2 → Part 3 → Part 4

For your Band 8–9 target, a useful practice benchmark is approximately 35–40/40, with 37–40/40 being a strong target for consistent practice.

Part 1 — Everyday Social Situation​

Typical situation​

You usually hear a conversation between two speakers in an everyday context.

Examples:

  • Booking accommodation
  • Joining a club
  • Asking about a course
  • Renting a house
  • Making an appointment
  • Registering for a service
  • Asking about transportation
  • Enquiring about an event

Example​

A person calls a language school:

Student → asks about English courses Receptionist → explains course times, prices and registration

You might need to identify:

  • Name
  • Address
  • Telephone number
  • Date
  • Time
  • Price
  • Course type
  • Location

Difficulty​

Part 1 is generally the easiest section.

However, easy does not mean you should relax. For a Band 8–9 target, losing marks here because of spelling or careless mistakes can be costly.

3. Part 2 — Social / General Monologue

Part 2 normally involves one speaker.

The speaker may give information about:

  • A building
  • A tourist attraction
  • A local area
  • A museum
  • A university facility
  • A community event
  • A public service
  • A tour

Example​

You might hear:

A guide explaining the layout and facilities of a large museum.

Questions could ask you to identify:

  • Locations
  • Facilities
  • Directions
  • Opening times
  • Rules
  • Features

Common question type​

You may receive a map or plan.

For example:

                NORTH
                  ↑

        ┌───────────────┐
        │   Library     │
        │               │
        ├───────┬───────┤
        │ Café  │       │
        │       │ Hall  │
        └───┬───┴───────┘
            │
          Entrance

You need to follow the speaker's directions carefully.

Words such as:

  • opposite
  • next to
  • behind
  • beside
  • at the end of
  • on the left
  • on the right
  • directly across from
  • in the corner

become important.

4. Part 3 — Academic Discussion

Part 3 is usually more challenging.

You normally hear two or more speakers discussing an academic topic.

For example:

  • Two students discussing a project
  • Students talking with a professor
  • Students discussing research
  • A tutor giving feedback
  • Students planning a presentation

Example​

Two university students discuss their research project.

They might talk about:

  • Research methodology
  • Previous studies
  • Problems with their research
  • Results
  • Data collection
  • Presentation structure

A tutor may correct or challenge their ideas.

Why Part 3 is difficult​

Speakers may:

  • Change their opinions
  • Disagree
  • Correct themselves
  • Interrupt each other
  • Give similar information
  • Use paraphrases

For example:

"Initially, we thought the survey should include 200 participants."

Later:

"Actually, after discussing it with our supervisor, we've decided that 150 would be sufficient."

The answer may therefore be 150, not 200.

5. Part 4 — Academic Lecture

Part 4 is usually the most difficult section.

You normally hear one speaker giving an academic lecture.

Possible topics include:

  • Biology
  • Environmental science
  • Psychology
  • History
  • Architecture
  • Technology
  • Archaeology
  • Economics
  • Education

Example​

You might hear a lecture about:

"The development of renewable energy technology."

The speaker could discuss:

  1. Historical development
  2. Current technology
  3. Advantages
  4. Problems
  5. Future developments

There is usually no conversation to help you recover if you lose track.

6. Why Part 4 Is Difficult

Part 4 requires you to listen for a long period while following a structured lecture.

The speaker may say:

"There are three major factors responsible for this change..."

Then discuss:

Factor 1 → Factor 2 → Factor 3

You need to understand the organization of the lecture.

Common signposting expressions include:

Starting a topic​

  • Let's begin with...
  • First of all...
  • To start with...
  • The first point is...

Moving to another point​

  • Now let's turn to...
  • Moving on to...
  • The next issue is...
  • Another important factor is...

Giving examples​

  • For example...
  • For instance...
  • A good example of this is...

Contrasting​

  • However...
  • On the other hand...
  • In contrast...
  • Whereas...

Concluding​

  • Finally...
  • To sum up...
  • In conclusion...
  • Overall...

Recognizing these signals makes Part 4 much easier.

7. The 40 Questions

The Listening test contains 40 questions.

Each correct answer normally receives 1 mark.

Therefore:

40 questions → 40 raw marks

Your raw score is converted to an IELTS Listening band score.

A commonly used approximate conversion is:

Correct answersApprox. Band
39–409
37–388.5
35–368
32–347.5
30–317
26–296.5
23–256
18–225.5
16–175

These boundaries can vary slightly between individual test versions, so treat them as approximate rather than guaranteed.

For your Band 8–9 target​

A useful practice target is:

Band 8 → around 35–36/40

Band 8.5 → around 37–38/40

Band 9 → around 39–40/40

So ideally, during practice you should aim for 37+ consistently, rather than merely reaching 35 once.

8. Major Question Types

IELTS Listening uses several question formats.

8.1 Multiple Choice​

You choose the correct answer from options.

Example:

Why did the student choose the course?

A. It was inexpensive. B. It was close to home. C. It had flexible hours. D. It was recommended by a friend.

The recording may mention all four ideas, but only one is the final answer.

9. Multiple Choice — The Main Trap

Suppose you hear:

"At first, I considered the evening course because it was cheaper. But after checking the timetable, I realized the weekend course would fit my schedule better."

The recording mentioned:

  • Evening course
  • Cheaper
  • Weekend course
  • Schedule

The answer is:

Weekend course

The earlier information was a distractor.

This is one of the most important skills for Band 8+.

10. Matching

You match information with options.

For example:

PersonOpinion
AnnaA
DavidB
SarahC

The recording may contain several people's opinions.

You need to identify who said what.

This can become difficult when speakers discuss the same topic.

11. Plan / Map / Diagram Labelling

You may need to label:

  • Buildings
  • Rooms
  • Streets
  • Facilities
  • Equipment
  • Locations

For example:

             Garden
                ↑
       ┌──────────────┐
       │   Building A │
       │              │
       ├───────┬──────┤
       │ Café  │ Hall │
       └───┬───┴──────┘
           │
        Entrance

The speaker might say:

"The café is directly to the left of the main hall."

You need to understand the spatial relationship.

12. Form Completion

You complete missing information.

Example:

Library Membership Form

InformationAnswer
Name______
Address______
Membership type______
Start date______
Fee£______

This is especially common in Part 1.

13. Note Completion

You complete notes based on the recording.

Example:

Renewable Energy​

  • Main advantage: ______
  • Major limitation: ______
  • Developed rapidly during the ______
  • Current research focuses on ______

The answer is usually a word or short phrase from the recording.

14. Table Completion

You complete missing information in a table.

Example:

ProductMaterialCost
AWood£20
B______£35
CMetal£42

You need to listen for the missing information.

15. Sentence Completion

You complete a sentence using information from the recording.

Example:

The museum was originally built in ______.

If the recording says:

"The museum was originally built in 1895."

Answer:

1895

16. Short-Answer Questions

You answer a question using a limited number of words.

Example:

What TWO facilities are available at the sports centre?

You might need:

Swimming pool

and

Gym

Pay very close attention to instructions such as:

NO MORE THAN TWO WORDS AND/OR A NUMBER

17. Word Limits Are Extremely Important

Suppose the instruction says:

NO MORE THAN TWO WORDS

And the answer is:

the central library

That's three words.

So it would be marked incorrect if the expected answer requires all three.

You must carefully check:

  • ONE WORD ONLY
  • NO MORE THAN TWO WORDS
  • NO MORE THAN THREE WORDS
  • NO MORE THAN TWO WORDS AND/OR A NUMBER

18. Spelling Matters

Listening is not only about understanding the audio.

Your answer must also be correctly written.

For example:

❌ enviroment ✅ environment

❌ accomodation ✅ accommodation

❌ seperate ✅ separate

If you understand the word but spell it incorrectly, you can lose the mark.

19. Singular vs Plural

This is another common problem.

Suppose the recording says:

"There are several different types of vehicle."

If the answer requires:

vehicles

and you write:

vehicle

you may lose the mark.

Pay attention to:

  • -s
  • -es
  • singular/plural
  • countable nouns

20. Numbers

IELTS Listening frequently tests numbers.

You may hear:

  • 15
  • 50
  • 150
  • 15,000
  • 5.5
  • 55
  • 505

These can sound similar.

Example​

"The price is fifty pounds."

versus

"The price is fifteen pounds."

You need to distinguish the pronunciation.

21. Dates

Dates are also common.

Examples:

  • 15th March
  • March 15th
  • 1998
  • 2005
  • 21st September

Be careful when speakers correct themselves:

"The meeting is on Thursday—sorry, I mean Friday."

The final information is usually the relevant answer.

22. Names and Spelling

Part 1 frequently includes spelling.

Example:

"Could you spell your surname?"

The speaker might say:

"It's Rahman. R-A-H-M-A-N."

You need to write the correct spelling.

The alphabet is therefore important.

Especially practice distinguishing:

  • B / P
  • D / T
  • M / N
  • G / J
  • A / E
  • I / Y

23. Paraphrasing

This is one of the most important Band 8–9 skills.

The question rarely repeats exactly what the speaker says.

For example:

Question​

The course is suitable for people who ______.

Audio​

"This program is designed primarily for individuals who have little previous experience."

The answer may be:

have little previous experience

The question used:

suitable for

The audio used:

designed primarily for

You need to recognize equivalent meanings.

24. Common Paraphrasing Patterns

QuestionAudio
cheapinexpensive
expensivecostly
difficultchallenging

improveenhance

purchasebuy

Don't try to memorize thousands of synonyms mechanically.

Instead, develop the ability to recognize meaning changes and paraphrases in context.

25. Distractors

Distractors are one of the biggest reasons students lose marks.

Consider:

"The conference was originally planned for Tuesday, but because of the weather, it was moved to Wednesday."

If the question asks:

When will the conference take place?

Answer:

Wednesday

Not Tuesday.

The speaker intentionally gave both pieces of information.

26. Self-Correction

Speakers frequently correct themselves.

Example:

"The meeting starts at 10... actually, sorry, it's at 10:30."

Correct answer:

10:30

Watch for phrases such as:

  • actually
  • sorry
  • I mean
  • rather
  • no, that's not right
  • let me correct that
  • or perhaps
  • I should say

27. Prediction Before Listening

For Band 8–9, don't simply wait for the answer.

Before the recording begins, analyze the question.

Suppose you see:

The library opens at ______ on weekdays.

You can predict:

a time

Therefore, you are listening for something like:

8:00 8:30 9:00

This reduces the amount of information your brain needs to process.

28. Predict the Grammar

You can often predict the type of answer.

Example​

The building was constructed in ______.

Likely:

a year

Example​

The main reason for the change was ______.

Likely:

a noun / noun phrase

Example​

The researchers decided to ______.

Likely:

a verb

This is extremely useful.

29. Read Ahead

Before each section begins, use the available time to examine the upcoming questions.

Don't read every word equally.

Look for:

  • Keywords
  • Names
  • Numbers
  • Places
  • Dates
  • Question types
  • Word limits

For example:

The new building will contain ______ classrooms and ______ laboratories.

Immediately notice:

two numbers

This prepares your brain for what to listen for.

30. Follow the Question Order

In most Listening question sets, answers generally follow the order in which information appears in the recording.

For example:

Question 1 → Information A
Question 2 → Information B
Question 3 → Information C
Question 4 → Information D

So if you have just heard the answer to Question 3, you should generally focus on Question 4 rather than continuing to search for Question 3.

31. Don't Get Stuck

This is extremely important.

Suppose you miss Question 15.

Don't spend the next 30 seconds thinking:

"What was the answer?"

You may then miss:

  • Q16
  • Q17
  • Q18

Instead:

Accept the loss → move forward → focus on the next question.

One missed answer should not become four missed answers.

32. The Listening Test Is a Concentration Test

You need to maintain concentration for the entire recording.

Your mental process should look like:

Read question
      ↓
Predict answer
      ↓
Listen
      ↓
Recognize paraphrase
      ↓
Identify answer
      ↓
Write answer
      ↓
Move to next question

You repeat this process continuously.

33. Paper-Based vs Computer-Delivered IELTS

The Listening content is essentially the same, but the interface differs.

Paper-based​

You write answers on paper.

There is a dedicated 10-minute period after the recordings to transfer your answers to the answer sheet.

Computer-delivered​

You enter answers on the computer.

The answer-review/transfer process is different, so you should practice using the same format as the test you intend to take.

34. Your Band 8–9 Target

Since your target is Band 8–9, don't prepare merely to "understand English."

You need to train these specific skills:

Skill 1 — Prediction​

Predict:

  • answer type
  • grammar
  • possible vocabulary

Skill 2 — Paraphrase recognition​

Understand that:

"The price has gone up"

can mean:

"The cost has increased."

Skill 3 — Distractor detection​

Recognize:

initial information → correction → final answer

Skill 4 — Spelling​

Develop extremely reliable spelling.

Skill 5 — Numbers​

Practice:

  • dates
  • prices
  • percentages
  • decimals
  • telephone numbers
  • years

Skill 6 — Concentration​

Don't mentally translate every sentence into your native language.

Skill 7 — Question tracking​

Know exactly where you are in the test.

35. Recommended Band 8–9 Strategy

Before the recording​

Spend the preparation time:

1. Read questions

↓

2. Underline important words

↓

3. Identify question type

↓

4. Predict the answer

↓

5. Predict synonyms/paraphrases

↓

6. Look at the next questions

During the recording​

Use:

Listen → Identify → Write → Move

Don't try to understand every single word.

Your goal is to extract the information required by the questions.

After each section​

Don't spend too much time worrying about previous answers.

Immediately prepare for the next section.

36. A Strong Practice Method

Instead of simply doing one full Listening test every day, use a combination of techniques.

Stage 1 — Full test​

Complete a complete 40-question test under realistic conditions.

Record your score.

Example:

32/40

Stage 2 — Error analysis​

For every incorrect answer, determine why you lost the mark.

Create categories:

ErrorExample
Spellingaccomodation
Pluralstudent → students
Distractorchose first information
Vocabularydidn't know word
Paraphrasedidn't recognize meaning
Concentrationmissed information
Predictionexpected wrong answer type
Number15 vs 50
Question trackinglost position

This is much more useful than simply doing more tests.

For a Band 8–9 target, maintain a listening error log.

Example:

After several tests, you'll see your actual weaknesses.

38. Shadowing Practice

Another useful technique is shadowing.

Listen to a short section of English and repeat the speaker's words immediately.

For example:

Audio:

"The research was conducted over a period of six months."

You repeat:

"The research was conducted over a period of six months."

This can improve:

  • Listening speed
  • Pronunciation awareness
  • Connected speech recognition
  • Concentration
  • Ability to process spoken English

39. Dictation Practice

Dictation is particularly useful if you are struggling with accuracy.

Listen to a short sentence and write exactly what you hear.

Then compare your version with the transcript.

For example:

Audio

"The number of visitors increased significantly."

You write:

"The number of visitors increased significantly."

Then check:

  • Did you miss of?
  • Did you hear visitors?
  • Did you recognize increased?
  • Did you hear significantly?

This helps identify exactly what your ears are missing.

40. The Biggest Difference Between Band 6 and Band 8+

A Band 6 student might understand:

"The course starts in September."

A Band 8 student is more likely to notice:

"The course was originally scheduled to begin in September, but due to the renovation work, it has been postponed until October."

The second student is better at detecting:

  • Changes
  • Corrections
  • Paraphrases
  • Important details
  • Distractors
  • Relationships between ideas

That is the type of listening ability you should develop.

41. Band 8–9 Checklist

Before your actual test, you should be comfortable with:

Vocabulary​

  • Common academic vocabulary
  • Everyday vocabulary
  • Synonyms/paraphrases
  • Common collocations

Accuracy​

  • Spelling
  • Singular/plural
  • Numbers
  • Dates
  • Names
  • Addresses

Question types​

  • Multiple choice
  • Matching
  • Map/plan
  • Form completion
  • Note completion
  • Table completion
  • Sentence completion
  • Short answers

Listening skills​

  • Prediction
  • Distractor detection
  • Following corrections
  • Following signposting
  • Recognizing paraphrases
  • Maintaining concentration
  • Moving on after a missed answer

42. The Ideal Band 8–9 Mindset

Don't think:

"I need to understand every word."

Think:

"I need to accurately identify the information required by each question."

That's a major difference.

Your goal is not to translate the entire recording. Your goal is to follow the speaker, anticipate the information, recognize the relevant answer, and record it accurately.

The core strategy​

Predict → Listen → Recognize → Verify → Write → Move on

For a Band 8–9 target, aim to make 35–40 correct answers consistently in practice, with particular attention to eliminating careless errors.
