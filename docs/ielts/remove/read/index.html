IELTS Academic Reading Test — Complete Guide

1. IELTS Academic Reading at a Glance​

FeatureDetails
Total marks40
Answer transfer timeNo additional time
Question typesAround 10–14 types
Main skillsSkimming, scanning, understanding detail, inference, vocabulary, identifying arguments

Test Facts​

  • 1 mark per correct answer
  • No additional 10-minute answer-transfer period

Common Question Types​

  • Multiple Choice
  • True / False / Not Given
  • Yes / No / Not Given
  • Matching Headings
  • Matching Information
  • Matching Features
  • Sentence Completion
  • Summary Completion
  • Note Completion
  • Table Completion
  • Flow-chart Completion
  • Short Answer Questions

For Your Band 8–9 Goal 🎯​

Aim for approximately 37–40 correct answers out of 40, with 39–40/40 as a strong practice target. The key skills to develop are skimming, scanning, paraphrase recognition, precise comprehension, and avoiding IELTS distractors/traps.

Important​

Unlike Listening, there is no extra 10-minute period to transfer answers.

You have to:

Read → Answer → Check your answers within the same 60 minutes.

2. Structure of the Academic Reading Test

You will receive three long reading passages.

Passage 1​

Usually the most accessible passage.

It may discuss:

  • Science
  • History
  • Technology
  • Environment
  • Society
  • Education
  • Archaeology

Typical difficulty: ⭐⭐☆☆☆

Passage 2​

Usually more complex.

You may encounter:

  • Research findings
  • Scientific explanations
  • Social issues
  • Historical developments
  • Academic arguments

Typical difficulty: ⭐⭐⭐☆☆

Passage 3​

Usually the most challenging.

It often contains:

  • Complex arguments
  • Research studies
  • Theories
  • Multiple viewpoints
  • Abstract concepts
  • Difficult vocabulary

Typical difficulty: ⭐⭐⭐⭐☆

For a Band 8–9 target, Passage 3 is particularly important.

3. What Kind of Texts Appear?

IELTS Academic Reading uses texts taken from sources such as:

  • Books
  • Journals
  • Magazines
  • Newspapers
  • Academic publications
  • Research-related material

However, you do not need specialist knowledge of the topic.

For example, you might get a passage about:

"The development of artificial intelligence"

You aren't expected to already know AI.

The test is primarily checking whether you can understand information presented in the passage.

4. The Four Main Reading Skills

To score Band 8–9, you need strong control over four major skills.

Skill 1 — Skimming​

Skimming means reading quickly to understand the general meaning.

You look for:

  • Main topic
  • Main idea
  • Paragraph purpose
  • Overall argument
  • Organization

For example:

Paragraph A → historical background Paragraph B → problems with the old method Paragraph C → new research Paragraph D → results

You don't need to understand every sentence.

5. Scanning

Scanning means searching the passage for specific information.

For example:

According to the passage, when was the first experiment conducted?

You would scan for:

  • Dates
  • Names
  • Numbers
  • Places
  • Technical terms
  • Keywords

Scanning is especially useful for:

  • Matching information
  • Short-answer questions
  • Sentence completion
  • Multiple choice

6. Understanding Meaning

IELTS frequently tests whether you understand what a sentence means, rather than whether you recognize the exact words.

For example:

Passage:

Researchers discovered that urban trees can significantly reduce local temperatures.

Question:

Urban trees have little effect on temperature.

You need to understand that:

significantly reduce = have a substantial effect

This is why vocabulary and paraphrasing are extremely important.

7. Recognizing Paraphrases

This is one of the most important Band 8–9 skills.

The question often does not use the same words as the passage.

Passage​

The number of people living in cities has increased dramatically.

Question​

Urban populations have experienced a ______ increase.

The passage says:

increased dramatically

The question may say:

experienced a rapid/substantial/considerable increase.

You need to recognize equivalent meaning.

8. IELTS Academic Reading Question Types

There are several common question types.

1. Multiple Choice​

You choose the correct answer from options such as:

A, B, C, D

Example:

What is the main purpose of the research?

A. To investigate climate change B. To develop a new technology C. To examine human behavior D. To compare two theories

Band 8–9 strategy​

Don't choose an answer simply because it contains words from the passage.

IELTS frequently creates distractors.

An incorrect option may contain the exact keyword but give the wrong meaning.

9. True / False / Not Given

You determine whether a statement is:

TRUE​

The statement agrees with the passage.

FALSE​

The statement contradicts the passage.

NOT GIVEN​

The passage does not provide enough information.

Example​

Passage:

The university introduced the program in 2015.

Statement:

The university introduced the program in 2015.

TRUE

Statement:

The university introduced the program in 2017.

FALSE

Statement:

The university's program was more successful than other universities' programs.

NOT GIVEN

The passage doesn't provide that comparison.

The biggest mistake​

Many students think:

"I can't find this information, so it must be FALSE."

That's incorrect.

If the passage doesn't tell you whether the statement is true or false:

NOT GIVEN

10. Yes / No / Not Given

This is similar to True/False/Not Given, but it is generally used when the passage contains opinions, claims, or views.

YES​

The writer agrees.

NO​

The writer disagrees.

NOT GIVEN​

The writer doesn't express an opinion on it.

11. Matching Headings

You receive headings such as:

A. The origins of modern transportation B. Problems with early designs C. A revolutionary discovery D. Future developments

Then you match headings to paragraphs.

Example:

Paragraph A → ?

The main challenge is to identify the main idea, not a minor detail.

Important Band 8–9 technique​

Ask:

"If I had to give this paragraph a one-sentence title, what would it be?"

That usually leads you toward the correct heading.

12. Matching Information

You are given pieces of information and must identify which paragraph contains them.

For example:

Which paragraph mentions:

A. an unexpected discovery B. an unsuccessful experiment C. a financial problem D. a change in public opinion

You search the passage for the relevant information.

13. Matching Features

You may need to match information with:

  • Researchers
  • Scientists
  • Countries
  • Companies
  • Historical periods
  • Theories

Example:

StatementPerson
Developed the original theoryA
Challenged the theoryB
Conducted the experimentC

This requires careful tracking of names and claims.

14. Sentence Completion

You complete a sentence using words from the passage.

Example:

The experiment was conducted in ______.

If the passage says:

The experiment was conducted in London in 2012.

Answer:

London

Very important​

Follow the word limit.

If the instruction says:

NO MORE THAN TWO WORDS

You cannot write three words.

15. Summary Completion

You receive a summary with gaps.

Example:

Scientists discovered that ______ could improve the efficiency of the system.

You locate the relevant section and find the appropriate word(s).

Sometimes you select answers from a box.

16. Note Completion

Similar to summary completion, but information is presented as notes.

For example:

Advantages of the system

• Lower energy consumption • Reduced ______ • Greater efficiency

You locate the relevant part of the passage.

17. Table Completion

Information is organized into a table.

Example:

PeriodDevelopment
1950s______
1960sNew technology introduced
1970s______

You find the relevant information in the passage.

18. Flow-chart Completion

You complete stages in a process.

Example:

Raw material

↓

Processing

↓

______

↓

Final product

This is particularly useful when a passage explains a:

  • Scientific process
  • Manufacturing process
  • Natural process
  • Research procedure

19. Short Answer Questions

You answer questions using information from the passage.

Example:

What did researchers use to measure temperature?

Answer:

Sensors

Again, respect the word limit.

20. Word Limits Are Extremely Important

Suppose the instruction says:

NO MORE THAN THREE WORDS

You can write:

  • one word
  • two words
  • three words

But not four.

For example:

NO MORE THAN TWO WORDS

Correct:

climate change

Incorrect:

the effects of climate change

That's four words.

21. Reading Score Conversion

Your raw score is converted into an IELTS band score.

For Academic Reading, approximate conversions commonly used are:

Correct answers / 40Approx. Band
409.0
398.5
37–388.0
35–367.5
33–347.0
30–326.5
27–296.0

The exact conversion can vary slightly by test version.

For your Band 8–9 goal​

A useful target is:

37–40 / 40

That means you can afford only about 0–3 mistakes for the highest bands.

22. Band 8–9 Reading Requires More Than Vocabulary

A common misconception is:

"I need to know every difficult English word."

Not necessarily.

You need to be able to:

Understand context​

Understand what a word means in that sentence.

Recognize paraphrases​

For example:

increase → rise → grow → expand

Identify relationships​

Such as:

cause → effect

problem → solution

claim → evidence

opinion → counterargument

Ignore irrelevant information​

You don't need to understand every detail equally.

23. Recommended 60-Minute Strategy

For a Band 8–9 candidate, I recommend practicing something close to:

ActivityTime
Passage 115–17 min
Passage 218–20 min
Passage 323–25 min
Final checking2–4 min

Don't spend 25 minutes on Passage 1 just because it feels comfortable.

The later passage can require considerably more reasoning.

24. Should You Read the Entire Passage First?

There isn't one universally correct method.

For Band 8–9, a practical approach is:

Step 1​

Read the title.

Step 2​

Quickly skim the passage.

Pay attention to:

  • First sentence of paragraphs
  • Last sentence
  • Names
  • Dates
  • Numbers
  • Bold/italic terms
  • Technical vocabulary

Step 3​

Look at the questions.

Step 4​

Return to the relevant section.

Step 5​

Read the surrounding sentences carefully.

This is generally faster than carefully reading every sentence from beginning to end before looking at the questions.

25. Don't Read Like a Normal Book

This is important.

Normal reading:

Sentence → sentence → sentence → sentence

IELTS reading:

Question → keyword → location → context → answer

You're solving a problem, not reading for entertainment.

26. Keyword Strategy

Suppose the question says:

What caused the decline in bee populations?

Important keywords:

decline bee populations

But don't search only for exactly those words.

The passage might say:

Bee numbers have fallen substantially because of habitat destruction.

Here:

decline → fallen populations → numbers caused by → because of

This is why paraphrase recognition is essential.

27. The "Keyword + Context" Rule

Never choose an answer based on a keyword alone.

Instead:

Find the keyword​

↓

Read the surrounding sentence​

↓

Read 1–2 sentences before/after if necessary​

↓

Understand the meaning​

↓

Select the answer​

This prevents many traps.

28. Common IELTS Reading Traps

Trap 1 — Same words​

The question uses words directly from the passage.

That doesn't automatically make it correct.

Trap 2 — Extreme words​

Be careful with:

  • always
  • never
  • all
  • completely
  • only
  • must
  • every

If the passage says:

"Some researchers..."

and the question says:

"All researchers..."

That's a significant difference.

Trap 3 — Partial information​

The answer may be almost correct but contain one incorrect detail.

Trap 4 — Wrong paragraph​

You find relevant information but it answers a different question.

Trap 5 — Not Given​

Students often invent information based on their general knowledge.

Don't.

Your answer must come from the passage.

29. How to Handle Difficult Vocabulary

Suppose you encounter:

"The technology was subsequently abandoned."

Maybe you don't know "subsequently."

Look at the context.

If the previous sentence discusses development and the next sentence discusses discontinuation, you can infer:

subsequently ≈ afterward/later

You don't necessarily need a dictionary definition.

30. Vocabulary Categories You Should Build

For Band 8–9, focus on academic paraphrases.

Increase​

  • increase
  • rise
  • grow
  • expand
  • climb
  • surge

Decrease​

  • decrease
  • decline
  • fall
  • drop
  • diminish
  • reduce

Important​

  • important
  • significant
  • crucial
  • considerable
  • substantial

Cause​

  • cause
  • lead to
  • result in
  • contribute to
  • trigger
  • produce

Problem​

  • problem
  • issue
  • challenge
  • difficulty
  • obstacle
  • limitation

31. How to Practice for Band 8–9

Don't simply do hundreds of tests.

Instead, use this cycle:

Step 1 — Take a timed test​

Complete one reading test in 60 minutes.

Step 2 — Calculate your score​

Example:

31/40

Step 3 — Categorize every mistake​

For every wrong answer, identify the reason:

A. Vocabulary problem

B. Couldn't locate information

C. Misunderstood the sentence

D. Paraphrase problem

E. Question-type mistake

F. Careless mistake

G. Time pressure

Step 4 — Fix the specific weakness​

This is much more effective than simply doing another test.

33. A Strong Band 8–9 Preparation Plan

Phase 1 — Foundation​

Learn:

  • Skimming
  • Scanning
  • Paraphrasing
  • Question types
  • Context clues
  • Academic vocabulary

Phase 2 — Question-Type Training​

Practice each type separately.

For example:

Day 1: True/False/Not Given Day 2: Matching Headings Day 3: Multiple Choice Day 4: Sentence Completion Day 5: Matching Information Day 6: Summary Completion Day 7: Mixed practice

Phase 3 — Timed Tests​

Start doing complete:

40 questions / 60 minutes

Track your score.

Phase 4 — Error Analysis​

This is where you move from approximately Band 6–7 toward Band 8+.

Don't just ask:

"What was the correct answer?"

Ask:

"Why did I choose the wrong answer?"

34. Band 8–9 Target

If your ultimate target is Band 8–9, I would structure your preparation around these five priorities:

1. Paraphrase recognition​

★★★★★

2. Question-type mastery​

★★★★★

3. Fast information location​

★★★★★

4. Accuracy under time pressure​

★★★★★

5. Academic vocabulary​

★★★★☆

You don't need to understand every word in the passage.

You need to consistently locate, interpret, and verify the information that answers the questions.

35. Your Ideal Exam-Day Workflow

A practical workflow is:

60-minute timer

↓

Passage 1​

Skim → Questions → Locate → Verify → Answer

↓

Passage 2​

Skim → Questions → Locate → Verify → Answer

↓

Passage 3​

Skim → Questions → Locate → Verify → Answer

↓

Final 2–4 minutes​

Check:

  • Spelling
  • Singular/plural
  • Word limits
  • Grammar
  • Unanswered questions

The Most Important Rule​

For IELTS Academic Reading, remember:

The answer is in the passage—not in your general knowledge.

And for Band 8–9:

Don't search for identical words. Search for identical meaning.

That single change in mindset can make a major difference to your reading performance.
