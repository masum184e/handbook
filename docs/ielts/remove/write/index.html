Important​

You should not spend 30 minutes on Task 1 and 30 minutes on Task 2.

A practical strategy is:

Task 1 → 20 minutes Task 2 → 40 minutes

Because Task 2 carries approximately twice the weight of Task 1.

2. IELTS Academic Writing Task 1

Task 1 asks you to write a report describing, summarizing, or explaining visual information.

You will normally receive one of these:

Common Task 1 types​

  1. Line graph
  2. Bar chart
  3. Pie chart
  4. Table
  5. Multiple charts
  6. Process diagram
  7. Map
  8. Combination of charts/graphs

You need to write at least 150 words.

3. What Does Task 1 Ask You to Do?

The question usually gives you a visual and an instruction such as:

Summarise the information by selecting and reporting the main features, and make comparisons where relevant.

This means you should:

Identify​

  • What the visual represents
  • The time period
  • Units
  • Categories
  • Important changes

Select​

You do not need to describe every single number.

Instead, identify the most important:

  • trends
  • differences
  • similarities
  • highest values
  • lowest values
  • significant changes
  • exceptions

Compare​

IELTS expects you to make meaningful comparisons.

For example:

The number of students studying Computer Science was considerably higher than the figure for History.

4. The Best Structure for Task 1

For a Band 8–9 target, a very useful structure is:

Paragraph 1 — Introduction​

Paraphrase the question.

Paragraph 2 — Overview​

Give the main trends/features without too many numbers.

Paragraph 3 — Details 1​

Describe important data/features.

Paragraph 4 — Details 2​

Describe remaining important data/features and comparisons.

So:

Introduction → Overview → Details 1 → Details 2

5. Task 1 Introduction

The introduction should tell the reader what the visual shows.

Question​

The graph below shows the percentage of people who used public transport in three cities between 2000 and 2020.

A simple introduction:

The graph illustrates the percentage of people using public transportation in three cities from 2000 to 2020.

Notice that you paraphrased the original question.

Useful verbs​

Instead of repeatedly using shows, use:

  • illustrates
  • compares
  • presents
  • provides information about
  • gives information about
  • depicts

6. Task 1 Overview — The Most Important Part

The overview is extremely important.

It should summarize the main features of the visual.

You generally shouldn't include many specific numbers here.

For example:

Overall, public transport usage increased in all three cities, although the rate of growth varied considerably. City A experienced the most significant increase, while City C remained comparatively stable.

This tells the examiner:

  • what happened overall
  • which category changed most
  • which category changed least

For graphs, look for:​

  • Overall increase
  • Overall decrease
  • Fluctuation
  • Stability
  • Highest point
  • Lowest point
  • Major difference
  • Similar trends

7. Task 1 Body Paragraphs

The body paragraphs contain the supporting details.

Suppose you have:

YearCity ACity BCity C
200020%30%40%
201035%40%42%
202060%50%45%

You don't need to describe every number separately.

Instead:

In 2000, City C had the highest proportion of public transport users, at 40%, followed by City B and City A, at 30% and 20%, respectively. By 2020, however, City A had overtaken both other cities, reaching 60%.

This is much better than writing:

City A was 20% in 2000. City A was 35% in 2010. City A was 60% in 2020...

8. Language for Describing Trends

Increase​

  • increase
  • rise
  • grow
  • climb
  • go up
  • surge
  • jump

Example:

The number of users increased significantly.

Decrease​

  • decrease
  • decline
  • fall
  • drop
  • reduce
  • dip

Example:

The figure declined gradually after 2015.

No significant change​

  • remain stable
  • remain unchanged
  • stay constant
  • level off

Example:

The percentage remained relatively stable throughout the period.

Fluctuation​

  • fluctuate
  • vary
  • experience fluctuations

Example:

The figure fluctuated considerably between 2010 and 2020.

9. Useful Adverbs for Task 1

These help you describe the size of change.

Small change​

  • slightly
  • marginally
  • gradually
  • modestly

The figure increased slightly.

Large change​

  • significantly
  • considerably
  • substantially
  • dramatically
  • sharply

The number increased dramatically.

Speed​

  • rapidly
  • quickly
  • gradually
  • steadily

The percentage rose steadily throughout the period.

10. Useful Comparison Language

Comparison is one of the most important skills in Task 1.

Higher​

A was higher than B.

Lower​

A was lower than B.

Similar​

The figures for A and B were similar.

Difference​

There was a considerable difference between A and B.

Approximately​

The figure was approximately twice as high as that of B.

Ranking​

A recorded the highest figure, followed by B and C.

Respectively​

The figures for A and B were 45% and 30%, respectively.

11. Task 1 Process Diagrams

A process diagram is different from a graph.

You may be asked to describe:

  • manufacturing
  • recycling
  • food production
  • natural processes
  • biological processes
  • industrial processes

For example:

Raw material → Processing → Manufacturing → Packaging → Distribution

Recommended structure​

Introduction

Paraphrase the process.

Overview

Describe:

  • number of stages
  • starting point
  • ending point
  • whether it is linear or cyclical

Body 1

Describe the first stages.

Body 2

Describe the later stages.

12. Process Vocabulary

Useful words include:

  • initially
  • first
  • subsequently
  • next
  • thereafter
  • following this
  • once
  • after
  • before
  • finally
  • at the final stage

Passive voice​

Process descriptions frequently require passive structures.

For example:

The raw materials are transported to the factory.

The material is then processed.

The finished products are packaged and distributed.

13. Task 1 Maps

Map questions ask you to describe changes to a location.

You may compare:

Past → Present

or

Present → Proposed future

You should identify changes such as:

  • buildings added
  • buildings removed
  • roads constructed
  • roads widened
  • facilities relocated
  • areas converted
  • trees planted
  • parking areas created

Useful vocabulary​

Location

  • north of
  • south of
  • east of
  • west of
  • in the centre
  • adjacent to
  • next to
  • opposite
  • near
  • on the eastern side

Changes

  • constructed
  • demolished
  • replaced
  • converted into
  • expanded
  • relocated
  • transformed
  • removed

Example:

The old library was demolished and replaced by a larger community centre.

14. IELTS Academic Writing Task 2

Task 2 is an essay.

You are given a topic and asked to discuss an issue, give your opinion, examine advantages and disadvantages, or discuss both sides.

You need to write at least 250 words.

Task 2 is worth twice as much as Task 1, so it deserves more preparation and time.

15. Common Task 2 Question Types

You should become comfortable with these major types.

1. Opinion essay​

To what extent do you agree or disagree?

or

Do you agree or disagree?

2. Discussion essay​

Discuss both views and give your own opinion.

3. Advantages and disadvantages​

What are the advantages and disadvantages of this development?

Sometimes:

Do the advantages outweigh the disadvantages?

4. Problems and solutions​

What problems does this cause, and what solutions can you suggest?

5. Two-part question​

For example:

Why is this happening? What can be done to address this issue?

6. Positive/negative development​

Is this a positive or negative development?

You need to explain your position with appropriate reasons and examples.

16. The Best Basic Structure for Task 2

For your Band 8–9 target, a strong general structure is:

Introduction → Body Paragraph 1 → Body Paragraph 2 → Conclusion

Sometimes you may need three body paragraphs, depending on the question.

17. Task 2 Introduction

A good introduction normally contains:

Sentence 1 — Background/paraphrase​

Introduce the topic.

Sentence 2 — Thesis​

Clearly state your position or explain what the essay will discuss.

For example, for an opinion question:

In recent years, online education has become increasingly common. Although this approach provides greater flexibility for learners, I believe that traditional classroom education remains important because it offers direct interaction and structured learning.

The examiner immediately knows your position.

18. Body Paragraph Structure

A very effective structure is:

Point → Explanation → Example → Result/Link​

For example:

Point

One major advantage of online education is its flexibility.

Explanation

Students can access lectures and learning materials from almost any location and can often study according to their own schedules.

Example

For example, a university student who works part-time can watch recorded lectures in the evening rather than attending a fixed class.

Result

Consequently, online learning can make education more accessible to people with other responsibilities.

This creates a logical paragraph instead of simply listing ideas.

19. Your Requested Band 8–9 Formula

Since you're targeting Band 8–9, you can use these frameworks.

Task 1​

Introduction → Overview → Main Details → Comparisons

or:

Paraphrase → Overall trend → Evidence → Comparison

Task 2​

Opinion → Reason → Explanation → Example → Result

For more complex arguments:

Point → Reason → Explanation → Example → Consequence → Qualification

20. Example of a Strong Body Paragraph

Topic:

Some people believe that university education should be free for everyone. Do you agree or disagree?

A developed paragraph could follow this pattern:

One argument in favour of free university education is that it can improve access to higher education. When tuition fees are reduced or eliminated, students from lower-income families may be less restricted by financial considerations when choosing whether to attend university. For example, a capable student from a disadvantaged background may be able to pursue an engineering degree if tuition costs are not a major barrier. This could increase social mobility and allow economies to benefit from a larger pool of highly educated workers.

Notice the progression:

Point → Reason → Explanation → Example → Result

21. What IELTS Examiners Assess

IELTS Writing is assessed using four criteria.

Each criterion contributes to your writing score.

1. Task Achievement — Task 1​

The examiner considers whether you:

  • address the task
  • describe the important features
  • provide an appropriate overview
  • support descriptions with relevant data
  • make appropriate comparisons

2. Task Response — Task 2​

The examiner considers whether you:

  • answer the question
  • present a clear position
  • develop your ideas
  • provide relevant supporting ideas
  • use appropriate examples
  • address all parts of the question

22. Coherence and Cohesion

This concerns how logically your writing is organized.

The examiner looks at:

  • paragraphing
  • logical progression
  • organization
  • linking
  • referencing
  • relationships between ideas

Weak​

Technology is useful. Technology is everywhere. Technology helps students. Technology can be expensive. Technology is important.

The ideas are disconnected.

Better​

Technology can significantly improve education because it gives students access to a wide range of learning resources. For example, online libraries and educational platforms allow learners to study beyond the limits of their physical classrooms. However, access to these resources can be expensive for students who cannot afford suitable devices or internet services.

The ideas connect logically.

23. Lexical Resource

This means vocabulary.

The examiner considers:

  • range of vocabulary
  • accuracy
  • appropriateness
  • word choice
  • collocations
  • ability to paraphrase

Don't simply memorize synonyms​

For example:

important → significant → crucial → vital → essential

Using different words incorrectly can lower your score.

The goal is:

accurate vocabulary > complicated vocabulary

24. Grammatical Range and Accuracy

The examiner considers:

  • grammatical accuracy
  • sentence structures
  • complex sentences
  • simple sentences
  • punctuation
  • variety

You should be able to use:

Simple sentence​

Technology has changed education.

Compound sentence​

Technology has changed education, and students now have access to more resources.

Complex sentence​

Although technology has made education more accessible, it can also create challenges for students who lack reliable internet access.

Relative clause​

Students who have access to online resources can study more independently.

Conditional​

If governments invest in digital infrastructure, more students may benefit from online education.

25. Band 8–9 Does NOT Mean Using Extremely Difficult Words

This is a common misconception.

Compare:

❌

The proliferation of technologically mediated pedagogical methodologies has precipitated an unprecedented paradigm shift...

This sounds unnecessarily complicated.

Better:

The rapid development of technology has significantly changed the way people learn.

The second sentence is clearer and more natural.

For Band 8–9, focus on:

Precision + flexibility + natural expression + grammatical control

26. Word Count

Task 1​

Minimum:

150 words

Task 2​

Minimum:

250 words

Going below the minimum can negatively affect your score.

However, writing extremely long answers is not automatically better.

A practical target is approximately:

Task 1​

160–190 words

Task 2​

260–320 words

The exact length is not a requirement; these are practical targets that leave room for development without encouraging unnecessary repetition.

27. Time Management

You have 60 minutes.

A useful plan:

Task 1 — 20 minutes​

2–3 min: Analyze question 2–3 min: Identify main features 12–14 min: Write 2–3 min: Check

Task 2 — 40 minutes​

5 min: Analyze question + brainstorm 28–30 min: Write 5–7 min: Check

28. What to Check in the Last Few Minutes

Look specifically for:

Grammar​

  • subject–verb agreement
  • articles
  • singular/plural
  • verb tense
  • prepositions

Vocabulary​

  • incorrect word choice
  • repeated words
  • spelling
  • unnatural expressions

Organization​

  • clear paragraphs
  • clear topic sentences
  • logical progression

Task completion​

Ask yourself:

Did I answer every part of the question?

This is particularly important for Task 2 two-part questions.

29. Common Mistakes

Mistake 1 — Memorized essays​

Memorizing a complete essay and forcing it onto different questions can produce irrelevant writing.

Instead, learn:

  • structures
  • useful vocabulary
  • argument-development techniques
  • sentence patterns

Mistake 2 — No clear opinion​

For an opinion essay, your position should be clear.

Don't make the examiner guess what you think.

Mistake 3 — Writing every number in Task 1​

Task 1 is not a data transcription exercise.

Select the most important information.

Mistake 4 — No overview in Task 1​

This is a major problem.

Always identify the overall pattern.

Mistake 5 — One idea per paragraph without development​

Instead of:

Technology is useful. It saves time. It is convenient.

Develop the idea:

One major advantage of technology is that it saves time. Automated systems can perform repetitive tasks much faster than humans, allowing people to focus on more complex activities. For example, digital payment systems can process transactions within seconds, reducing the time customers spend waiting.

Mistake 6 — Excessive linking words​

Don't write:

Firstly, moreover, furthermore, however, therefore, consequently...

in every paragraph.

Cohesion should come from logical relationships, not from inserting linking words everywhere.

30. Band 8–9 Writing Mindset

For your target score, focus on these five things:

1. Answer the exact question​

Don't write a memorized essay about a general topic.

2. Develop ideas​

Don't just state an opinion.

Explain:

Why? → How? → Example? → What consequence?

3. Make comparisons in Task 1​

Don't just report individual values.

4. Use flexible grammar​

Mix:

  • simple sentences
  • compound sentences
  • complex sentences
  • relative clauses
  • conditionals
  • concessive clauses

5. Prioritize accuracy​

A natural, accurate sentence is better than an unnecessarily complicated sentence.

31. Complete IELTS Academic Writing Strategy

Your overall approach can be:

                IELTS ACADEMIC WRITING
                         │
             ┌───────────┴───────────┐
             │                       │
          TASK 1                  TASK 2
             │                       │
        20 minutes              40 minutes
             │                       │
     ┌───────┴───────┐        ┌──────┴──────┐
     │               │        │             │
 Visual Information  │      Essay        Essay Type
     │               │        │             │
 Introduction        │    Introduction   Position
 Overview             │    Body 1        Arguments
 Details 1            │    Body 2        Examples
 Details 2            │    Conclusion    Conclusion
     │                         │
     └────────────┬────────────┘
                  │
        Check Grammar + Vocabulary
        + Coherence + Task Response

A simple formula to remember​

Task 1​

What does it show? → What is the overall picture? → What are the important details? → How do they compare?

Task 2​

What is my position? → Why? → How? → Example? → What does this mean?

For a Band 8–9 goal, mastering these structures is more useful than memorizing hundreds of phrases. The next step should be practicing each Task 1 type and each Task 2 question type separately, then gradually doing full 60-minute writing tests.
