IELTS Listening​

Duration​

Approximately 30 minutes.

The Listening test contains 40 questions.

You listen to four recordings.

Part 1​

Usually a conversation between two people in an everyday situation.

Example:

Someone is asking about accommodation.

You might need to identify:

  • name
  • address
  • telephone number
  • date
  • price
  • location

Part 2​

Usually a monologue in an everyday/social context.

For example:

A person explaining facilities in a community center.

Part 3​

Usually a conversation involving two or more people, often in an educational context.

For example:

Two students discussing a university project with a tutor.

Part 4​

Usually an academic monologue.

For example:

A university lecture about environmental science.

This is often one of the more challenging sections because there is usually one speaker and relatively dense information.

Listening Question Types

You may encounter:

1. Multiple choice​

You choose the correct option.

2. Matching​

Match information with:

  • people
  • places
  • categories
  • opinions

3. Plan/map/diagram labeling​

You identify locations or parts.

4. Form/note/table/summary completion​

You fill in missing information.

5. Sentence completion​

Complete a sentence using information from the recording.

6. Short-answer questions​

Answer using information from the audio.

The exact raw-score conversion can vary slightly by test version, but approximately:

For Band 8, you should generally aim for around 35–36/40.

For Band 9, aim for 39–40/40.

How to Prepare for Band 8–9 Listening

Don't only listen to IELTS recordings.

Use:

  • university lectures
  • interviews
  • documentaries
  • podcasts
  • news discussions
  • academic presentations

Your goal should be to understand different accents and natural speech.

Pay particular attention to:

Signposting​

Speakers often say:

However...

On the other hand...

Moving on to...

The important point is...

What I mean by this is...

These phrases help you follow the structure.

Distractors​

IELTS sometimes gives you information that sounds correct initially and then changes it.

For example:

"The meeting was originally scheduled for Monday, but it has now been moved to Wednesday."

The answer is Wednesday, not Monday.

3. IELTS Reading

Reading lasts 60 minutes.

There are:

40 questions

You normally have:

  • 3 passages in Academic Reading
  • 3 sections in General Training Reading

Academic Reading

Academic passages may come from topics such as:

  • science
  • technology
  • history
  • psychology
  • environment
  • education
  • biology
  • economics

You don't necessarily need specialist knowledge.

The important skill is finding and interpreting information from the text.

Major Reading Question Types

1. Multiple Choice​

Choose the correct answer.

2. True / False / Not Given​

You must distinguish between:

TRUE

The statement agrees with the passage.

FALSE

The statement contradicts the passage.

NOT GIVEN

The passage does not provide enough information.

This is one of the most important distinctions in IELTS Reading.

3. Yes / No / Not Given​

Similar idea, but generally used for opinions or claims.

4. Matching Headings​

You match headings to paragraphs.

This tests whether you understand the main idea.

5. Matching Information​

You identify which paragraph contains particular information.

6. Matching Features​

You match information with:

  • people
  • theories
  • places
  • dates
  • discoveries

7. Sentence Completion​

You complete sentences using words from the passage.

8. Summary Completion​

You complete a summary.

9. Diagram Label Completion​

You identify parts of a diagram.

Reading Strategy for Band 8–9

Don't try to understand every word.

Instead:

Step 1 — Read the question​

Identify important keywords.

Step 2 — Predict possible synonyms​

For example:

Question:

The project was extremely expensive.

Possible passage wording:

The initiative required substantial financial investment.

"expensive" → "substantial financial investment"

Step 3 — Scan the passage​

Find the relevant area.

Step 4 — Read around the answer​

Don't simply match identical words.

IELTS frequently uses paraphrasing.

The exact conversion can vary between tests.

For Band 8, therefore, you should train toward 35–36+ correct answers consistently.

4. IELTS Writing

Writing lasts:

60 minutes

There are two tasks.

Task 1​

Task 2​

Task 2 is worth more than Task 1 in the overall Writing score.

IELTS Academic Writing Task 1

You are given a visual such as:

  • line graph
  • bar chart
  • pie chart
  • table
  • process
  • map
  • combination of visuals

You normally write at least 150 words.

You should spend approximately:

20 minutes

on Task 1.

Task 1 Structure

A strong structure is:

Introduction​

Paraphrase the question.

Overview​

Give the most important overall trends/features.

Body Paragraph 1​

Explain important details.

Body Paragraph 2​

Explain remaining important details/comparisons.

Example

Suppose the graph shows smartphone ownership from 2010–2020.

Weak overview:

Smartphone ownership increased.

Better:

Overall, smartphone ownership increased substantially over the period, with the most pronounced growth occurring after 2015.

The second version demonstrates better:

  • overview
  • vocabulary
  • precision
  • grammatical control

Academic Task 1 — Important Language

Increase​

  • increase
  • rise
  • grow
  • climb
  • surge
  • increase substantially

Decrease​

  • decrease
  • decline
  • fall
  • drop
  • decline significantly

Stability​

  • remain stable
  • remain unchanged
  • level off
  • remain relatively constant

Comparison​

  • whereas
  • while
  • compared with
  • in contrast
  • respectively
  • considerably higher than

IELTS General Training Task 1

You normally write a letter.

It can be:

  • formal
  • semi-formal
  • informal

For example:

Write a letter to your landlord about a problem with your apartment.

IELTS Writing Task 2

This is an essay.

You normally write at least 250 words.

A strong Band 8–9 response usually has:

Introduction​

  • paraphrase topic
  • clear position/thesis

Body Paragraph 1​

  • main idea
  • explanation
  • example
  • consequence/extension

Body Paragraph 2​

  • main idea
  • explanation
  • example
  • consequence/extension

Conclusion​

  • summarize position
  • don't introduce a completely new argument

Your preferred structure can be:

Introduction​

Background → Paraphrase → Thesis

Body​

Point → Explanation → Example → Result

Conclusion​

Summary → Final position

This is particularly useful because it prevents your essay from becoming a collection of disconnected ideas.

IELTS Writing Assessment

Writing is assessed using four criteria:

1. Task Response​

Are you actually answering the question?

For Band 8–9:

  • address all parts
  • develop ideas
  • explain ideas clearly
  • provide relevant examples
  • maintain a clear position

2. Coherence and Cohesion​

This concerns how logically your writing is organized.

The examiner looks at:

  • paragraphing
  • progression
  • linking
  • referencing
  • logical relationships

Don't simply use:

Firstly, secondly, moreover, furthermore, in addition...

in every paragraph.

Natural cohesion is better.

3. Lexical Resource

This means vocabulary.

Band 8–9 vocabulary is not about using complicated words everywhere.

Compare:

This problem is very big.

with:

This issue has become increasingly significant.

But using unnecessarily complicated words can actually hurt clarity.

The goal is:

precise + natural + appropriate vocabulary.

4. Grammatical Range and Accuracy

You should demonstrate a mixture of:

  • simple sentences
  • compound sentences
  • complex sentences
  • relative clauses
  • conditionals
  • concessive clauses
  • participle structures

For example:

Although technology has transformed the way people communicate, excessive reliance on digital platforms can weaken face-to-face interaction.

This demonstrates more grammatical range than:

Technology is useful. People use it every day. It helps communication.

But complexity must remain accurate and natural.

5. IELTS Speaking

Speaking lasts approximately:

11–14 minutes

There are three parts.

Speaking Part 1

Approximately:

4–5 minutes

The examiner asks questions about familiar topics.

Examples:

Home​

Where do you live?

Study​

What do you study?

Hobbies​

What do you do in your free time?

Technology​

How often do you use your phone?

Food​

What kind of food do you enjoy?

Part 1 Answer Strategy

A useful structure is:

Answer → Reason → Example​

For example:

Question:

Do you enjoy reading?

Answer:

Yes, I do.

Reason:

I find reading useful because it allows me to learn about subjects that I'm interested in.

Example:

For example, I often read articles about artificial intelligence and computer science in my spare time.

This produces a much stronger response than:

Yes, I like reading.

Speaking Part 2

This is the cue-card section.

You receive a topic and have:

1 minute to prepare

Then you speak for:

1–2 minutes

The examiner may give you several bullet points.

Example:

Describe a useful piece of technology you own.

You might need to discuss:

  • what it is
  • when you bought it
  • how you use it
  • why it is useful

Part 2 Structure for Band 8–9

A very useful framework is:

Introduction​

Introduce the topic.

Background​

Explain when/where/how it happened.

Details​

Describe the important aspects.

Example​

Give a specific situation.

Feelings / Importance​

Explain why it matters to you.

Conclusion​

Finish naturally.

Example Framework

Instead of memorizing a complete answer, remember:

What → When → Where → How → Example → Why → Feeling → Conclusion

This allows you to adapt your answer to many cue cards.

Speaking Part 3

This is usually the most analytical section.

The examiner asks broader questions related to Part 2.

For example:

Part 2:

Describe a useful piece of technology.

Part 3:

How has technology changed education?

Do you think children depend too much on technology?

What technological developments might affect education in the future?

Part 3 Band 8–9 Structure

Your structure:

Opinion → Reason → Explanation → Example → Contrast/Qualification

For example:

I think technology has significantly improved access to education. One reason is that students can now access high-quality educational materials regardless of where they live. For example, online platforms allow students to watch university lectures and use digital textbooks without physically attending a university. However, this doesn't mean that traditional classrooms will become unnecessary, because direct interaction with teachers still plays an important role in learning.

That's much stronger than simply:

Yes, technology is good because it helps students.

IELTS Speaking Assessment

Speaking is assessed using four criteria.

1. Fluency and Coherence​

Can you speak continuously and organize your ideas?

Band 8 requires very strong fluency.

You don't need to speak extremely fast.

Natural fluency > speed.

2. Lexical Resource

You need:

  • wide vocabulary
  • precise vocabulary
  • natural collocations
  • ability to paraphrase
  • flexibility

For example:

Instead of repeatedly saying:

very important

you might use:

highly significant particularly important essential a key factor

depending on context.

3. Grammatical Range and Accuracy

You should demonstrate both:

range

and

accuracy.

Don't try to make every sentence extremely complex.

4. Pronunciation

The examiner considers whether your speech is easy to understand.

Important areas include:

  • individual sounds
  • word stress
  • sentence stress
  • intonation
  • rhythm
  • connected speech

You do not need a British or American accent.

The goal is:

clear, intelligible pronunciation.

Band 7 vs Band 8 vs Band 9

This distinction is extremely important.

Band 7​

Generally:

  • good vocabulary
  • generally clear organization
  • some complex grammar
  • some errors
  • can discuss familiar and unfamiliar topics

Band 8​

Generally:

  • wider and more precise vocabulary
  • strong organization
  • flexible grammar
  • very good fluency
  • errors are relatively infrequent
  • ideas are developed effectively

Band 9​

Very high-level control:

  • highly natural communication
  • extremely flexible language
  • sophisticated vocabulary used appropriately
  • excellent grammatical control
  • effortless organization
  • pronunciation is consistently easy to understand

Overall IELTS Score

Your four scores are averaged.

For example:

SkillScore
Listening8.5
Reading8.5
Writing7.5
Speaking8.0

Average:

8.125

IELTS applies its rounding rules, resulting in an overall Band 8.0.

IELTS Band 8–9 Vocabulary Strategy

Don't build a vocabulary list like:

good = excellent = exceptional = outstanding = phenomenal

and memorize it.

Instead, learn collocations and context.

For example:

Education​

  • academic performance
  • higher education
  • educational inequality
  • access to education
  • practical skills
  • academic achievement

Technology​

  • technological advancement
  • digital infrastructure
  • artificial intelligence
  • technological innovation
  • digital literacy
  • automation

Environment​

  • climate change
  • carbon emissions
  • renewable energy
  • environmental degradation
  • sustainable development
  • natural resources

This is much more useful for IELTS.

The Most Important Skill: Paraphrasing

IELTS repeatedly tests your ability to understand different ways of expressing the same idea.

For example:

Question:

Many people believe that technology has improved communication.

Possible paraphrase:

It is widely argued that technological developments have enhanced the way people communicate.

Notice:

many people believe

→ it is widely argued

technology

→ technological developments

improved

→ enhanced

communication

→ the way people communicate

Band 8–9 Grammar

You should become comfortable with:

Complex sentences​

Although online education is convenient, it cannot completely replace face-to-face interaction.

Relative clauses​

Students who have access to reliable technology can benefit from online resources.

Conditionals​

If governments invest more heavily in public transport, traffic congestion could be reduced.

Concessions​

While technology offers considerable benefits, it also creates new challenges.

Cause and effect​

The rapid growth of urban populations has contributed to increased pressure on housing.

Passive voice​

A significant proportion of household waste is recycled.

A Strong IELTS Preparation Plan

If your target is Band 8–9, I would divide preparation into four stages.

Stage 1 — Build English Foundation​

Focus on:

  • grammar
  • vocabulary
  • pronunciation
  • reading
  • listening

Don't immediately start doing full IELTS tests every day.

Stage 2 — Learn IELTS Techniques

Learn:

Listening​

  • predicting answers
  • identifying keywords
  • recognizing paraphrases
  • handling distractors

Reading​

  • skimming
  • scanning
  • keyword identification
  • paraphrasing
  • True/False/Not Given

Writing​

  • essay structures
  • argument development
  • cohesion
  • vocabulary
  • grammar

Speaking​

  • developing answers
  • giving examples
  • extending ideas
  • expressing opinions
  • comparing
  • qualifying opinions

Stage 3 — Intensive Practice

A possible weekly schedule:

DayMain Focus
SaturdayListening + vocabulary
SundayReading
MondayWriting Task 1
TuesdaySpeaking Part 1 + 2
WednesdayWriting Task 2
ThursdaySpeaking Part 3
FridayFull mock test + error analysis

The error analysis is extremely important.

Don't just record:

Reading: 32/40

Instead record:

QuestionErrorReason
5WrongMisread question
12WrongVocabulary
19WrongNot Given confusion
27WrongTime management

Then you know exactly what to improve.

Stage 4 — Band 8–9 Optimization

At this stage, your focus changes.

You aren't simply learning English.

You are reducing:

  • grammar errors
  • pronunciation problems
  • repetition
  • weak vocabulary
  • irrelevant ideas
  • poor paragraph organization
  • hesitation
  • misunderstanding of questions

Your goal becomes consistency.

Recommended Daily Routine

If you can study around 2–3 hours per day:

30 minutes — Listening​

Listen to authentic English and IELTS material.

30 minutes — Reading​

Read an article or IELTS passage.

30 minutes — Vocabulary + Grammar​

Learn vocabulary in context.

30 minutes — Speaking​

Record yourself answering questions.

30–60 minutes — Writing​

Alternate between Task 1 and Task 2.

The Biggest Mistakes Band 8 Candidates Make

1. Memorizing answers​

This can make your speaking unnatural.

2. Using unnecessarily complicated vocabulary​

Sophisticated ≠ difficult.

3. Using linking words excessively​

Every sentence doesn't need:

Moreover, furthermore, however, consequently...

4. Writing without answering the exact question​

Task Response is critical.

5. Giving undeveloped ideas​

A claim without explanation isn't enough.

6. Ignoring pronunciation​

Knowing vocabulary doesn't mean you can communicate it clearly.

7. Practicing without analyzing mistakes​

Practice alone isn't enough.

Practice → Analyze → Correct → Practice again

is much more effective.

Your Band 8–9 Answer Framework

Since you're targeting Band 8–9, a useful framework across the speaking sections is:

Part 1​

Answer → Reason → Example

Part 2​

Introduction → Background → Details → Example → Feelings/Importance → Conclusion

Part 3​

Opinion → Reason → Explanation → Example → Contrast/Qualification

These frameworks are useful because they help you develop ideas naturally without memorizing scripts.

One important point​

For Band 8–9, IELTS is not primarily a vocabulary test.

A candidate who knows 5,000 sophisticated words but makes frequent grammatical errors and gives poorly developed answers can score lower than someone with a smaller but precise, natural, flexible vocabulary.

Think of the target as:

Accuracy + Fluency + Flexibility + Coherence + Natural English

rather than simply:

Difficult vocabulary = high band.
