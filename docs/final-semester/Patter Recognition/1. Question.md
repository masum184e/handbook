<summary>(a) Steps to create a digital image from continuous sensed data</summary>

A digital image is obtained by converting a continuous scene into a discrete form.

**Image Acquisition**

* A sensor (camera, scanner, CCD sensor) captures the continuous light intensity from the object.

**Sampling (Spatial Digitization)**

* The continuous image is divided into a grid of pixels.
* This determines the image resolution.

Example:

Continuous image

```
□□□□□□□□
□□□□□□□□
□□□□□□□□
```

Each square = one pixel.

**Quantization (Intensity Digitization)**

* Each sampled pixel is assigned a finite intensity value.
* Example:

  * 8-bit image → 256 gray levels (0–255)
  * 16-bit image → 65536 gray levels

**Digital Image Formation**

The result is an M × N matrix.

Example

$$
\begin{bmatrix}
120 & 130 & 128\\
110 & 100 & 90\\
60 & 50 & 30
\end{bmatrix}
$$

**Final Flow Diagram**

```
Real Scene
     ↓
Image Sensor
     ↓
Sampling
     ↓
Quantization
     ↓
Digital Image
```

<summary>(b) Continuous Histogram Equalization</summary>

Assume continuous intensity values with the PDF

$$
P_r(r)=\frac{2r}{(L-1)^2} \qquad 0\le r\le (L-1)
$$

For a continuous image, the histogram equalization transformation is

$$
s=T(r)
$$

where

$$
T(r)=(L-1)\int_{0}^{r} P_r(w)dw
$$

Here,

* $P_r(w)$ = Probability Density Function (PDF)
* $L$ = Number of gray levels
* $r$ = Original intensity
* $s$ = Equalized intensity

Given

$$
P_r(r)=\frac{2r}{(L-1)^2}
$$

Replace ($r$) inside the integral by the dummy variable ($w$):

$$
P_r(w)=\frac{2w}{(L-1)^2}
$$

Substitute into the transformation:

$$
T(r) =(L-1)\int_{0}^{r}\frac{2w}{(L-1)^2}dw
$$

**Take Constants Outside the Integral**

Since $(L-1)^2$ is constant,

$$
T(r)
=\frac{L-1}{(L-1)^2}
\int_{0}^{r}2w dw
$$

Simplify

$$
=\frac{1}{L-1}
\int_{0}^{r}2w dw
$$

**Evaluate the Integral**

Recall,

$$
\int 2w dw=w^2
$$

Applying the limits:

$$
\int_{0}^{r}2w dw
=r^2-0^2
=r^2
$$

Therefore,

$$
T(r)=\frac{r^2}{L-1}
$$

**Write the Final Transformation Function**

Hence,

$$
\boxed{s=\frac{r^2}{L-1}}
$$

This is the histogram-equalization transformation.

**Verify the Transformation**

When (r=0)

$$
s=\frac{0^2}{L-1}=0
$$

Minimum intensity remains minimum.

When (r=L-1)

$$
s=\frac{(L-1)^2}{L-1}
=L-1
$$

Maximum intensity remains maximum.

Thus, the transformation correctly maps the intensity range ([0, L-1]) onto itself.

**Graph of the Transformation**

The transformation is

$$
s=\frac{r^2}{L-1}
$$

This is a **quadratic (parabolic) curve**.

```
s
↑
│                           •
│                      •
│                 •
│            •
│        •
│     •
│   •
│ •
└────────────────────────────→ r
0                           L−1
```

* Low-intensity pixels are compressed.
* High-intensity pixels are stretched.
* The resulting image has a more uniform histogram, improving contrast where needed.

Given

$$
P_r(r)=\frac{2r}{(L-1)^2}
$$

The histogram equalization transformation is

$$
s=(L-1)\int_0^r P_r(w),dw
$$

Substituting the PDF,

$$
s=(L-1)\int_0^r\frac{2w}{(L-1)^2},dw
$$

$$
=\frac{1}{L-1}\int_0^r2w,dw
$$

$$
=\frac{1}{L-1}\left[w^2\right]_0^r
$$

$$
=\frac{r^2}{L-1}
$$

Hence, the required transformation function is

$$
\boxed{s=\frac{r^2}{L-1}}
$$

<summary>(a) Median Filter</summary>

A median filter is a non-linear filter that replaces the center pixel with the median value of its neighboring pixels. It is effective in removing impulse (salt-and-pepper) noise while preserving image edges.

**Working Principle**

1. Place a **3×3 window (mask)** over the target pixel.
2. Collect the **9 pixel values** inside the window.
3. Sort them in **ascending order**.
4. Replace the center pixel with the **median (middle) value**.

For a **3×3 mask**, there are **9 values**, so the median is the **5th value** after sorting.

Given Image

$$
\begin{matrix}
12&13&22&26&32&24\\
34&\underline{123}&\underline{24}&\underline{100}&\underline{34}&22\\
14&15&13&32&31&21
\end{matrix}
$$

The underlined pixels (**123, 24, 100, 34**) must be filtered.

**Median for Pixel 123**

* Select the 3×3 neighborhood

$$
\begin{matrix}
12&13&22\\
34&123&24\\
14&15&13
\end{matrix}
$$

* List all values

$$
12, 13, 22, 34, 123, 24, 14, 15, 13
$$

* Sort

$$
12, 13, 13, 14, 15, 22, 24, 34, 123
$$

* Find the Median
    * There are 9 values.
    * The **5th value** is 15

$$
\boxed{123\rightarrow15}
$$

**Final Output**

The second row changes from

$$
34\quad123\quad24\quad100\quad34\quad22
$$

to

$$
34\quad15\quad24\quad31\quad31\quad22
$$

**Filtered values:**

$$
\boxed{

$$

So the filtered image becomes:

$$

<summary>(a) Explain the role of gamma correction in image enhancement. Compare the effects of using ( $\gamma < 1$ ) and ( $\gamma > 1$ ), and discuss why gamma correction is important for display devices such as monitors.</summary>

**Gamma correction** is a point processing (intensity transformation) technique used to adjust the brightness of an image by applying a nonlinear transformation.

The transformation is defined as:

$$
\boxed{s = c,r^\gamma}
$$

where:

* (r) = input pixel intensity (normalized between 0 and 1)
* (s) = output pixel intensity
* (c) = constant (usually (c=1))
* ($\gamma$) = gamma value

**Effect of Different Gamma Values**

Case 1: ( $\gamma < 1$ )

Suppose

$$
\gamma = 0.5
$$

Transformation:

$$
s=r^{0.5}=\sqrt{r}
$$

Example

If

$$
r=0.25
$$

then

$$
s=\sqrt{0.25}=0.5
$$

Since

$$
0.5>0.25
$$

the output becomes brighter.

Graph

```
Output Intensity
1.0 |                         ●
    |                    ●
    |                ●
    |            ●
    |         ●
    |      ●
    |   ●
0.0 +-------------------------------→ Input Intensity
      0                           1
```

(Curve lies **above** the diagonal.)

**Effect**

* Brightens dark regions.
* Reveals hidden details in shadows.
* Useful for underexposed images.

**Case 2: ( $\gamma > 1$ )**

Suppose

$$
\gamma = 2
$$

Transformation:

$$
s=r^2
$$

Example

If

$$
r=0.5
$$

then

$$
s=0.5^2=0.25
$$

Since

$$
0.25<0.5
$$

the image becomes darker.

Graph

```
Output Intensity
1.0 |                         ●
    |                      ●
    |                  ●
    |               ●
    |            ●
    |        ●
    |    ●
0.0 +-------------------------------→ Input Intensity
      0                           1
```

(Curve lies **below** the diagonal.)

**Effect**

* Darkens bright regions.
* Prevents overexposure.
* Preserves details in bright areas.

**Comparison**

| Property       | ( $\gamma <1$ )                                      | ( $\gamma >1$ )                                   |
| -------------- | -------------------------------------------------- | ----------------------------------------------- |
| Brightness     | Image becomes brighter                             | Image becomes darker                            |
| Dark Regions   | Enhanced                                           | Suppressed                                      |
| Bright Regions | Slightly saturated                                 | More detailed                                   |
| Curve Position | Above diagonal                                     | Below diagonal                                  |
| Applications   | Medical images, dark photographs, satellite images | Bright images, X-ray images, display correction |

**Why is Gamma Correction Important?**

Most display devices (CRT monitors, LCDs, LEDs, OLEDs) do **not** display brightness linearly.

Instead,

$$
I_{display}\propto V^{2.2}
$$

where:

* (V) = input voltage (or digital intensity)
* 2.2 ≈ gamma of many displays

Without correction:

* Dark images appear even darker.
* Brightness is distorted.
* Contrast is inaccurate.

To compensate, images are gamma corrected before display so that the displayed brightness appears natural.

**Example**

Suppose a monitor has

$$
\gamma = 2.2
$$

If the input intensity is

$$
0.5
$$

Displayed intensity becomes

$$
0.5^{2.2}\approx0.22
$$

Instead of appearing as **50% brightness**, it appears only **22% bright**.

Applying inverse gamma correction before display restores the intended brightness.

**Applications**

Gamma correction is widely used in:

* Digital cameras
* Mobile phone cameras
* Medical imaging
* Satellite image enhancement
* Television broadcasting
* Computer monitors
* Projectors
* Image editing software (Photoshop, GIMP)

**Advantages**

* Improves image contrast.
* Enhances visibility of details.
* Matches human visual perception.
* Corrects nonlinear display characteristics.
* Produces more natural-looking images.

**Disadvantages**

* Choosing an incorrect gamma value may over-brighten or over-darken the image.
* Excessive correction can reduce image quality and hide details.

**Final Exam Answer**

**Gamma correction** is a nonlinear intensity transformation used to adjust image brightness. It is defined by

$$
\boxed{s=c,r^\gamma}
$$

where (r) is the input intensity, (s) is the output intensity, (c) is a constant, and ($\gamma$) is the gamma value.

* For **( $\gamma < 1$ )**, the image becomes brighter because dark pixel values are increased, making shadow details more visible.
* For **( \gamma > 1 )**, the image becomes darker because bright pixel values are compressed, preserving details in bright regions.

Gamma correction is important because display devices have a nonlinear response to intensity values. Applying appropriate gamma correction compensates for this nonlinearity, ensuring that images are displayed with the intended brightness and contrast. It is widely used in cameras, monitors, televisions, medical imaging, and image processing applications.

**Summary Table**

| Gamma Value    | Effect              | Typical Use                  |
| -------------- | ------------------- | ---------------------------- |
| ( $\gamma < 1$ ) | Brightens the image | Dark or underexposed images  |
| ( $\gamma = 1$ ) | No change           | Original image               |
| ( $\gamma > 1$ ) | Darkens the image   | Bright or overexposed images |

<summary>(c) A forensic expert is working on an old, blurry crime scene image to identify a suspect.
Given the fields where digital image processing is used, which techniques could be applied
to enhance the image for better analysis?</summary>

A forensic expert can apply several **Digital Image Processing (DIP)** techniques to improve an old, blurry crime scene image:

1. **Image Enhancement**

   * Improves brightness and contrast.
   * Sharpens edges to make important details more visible.

2. **Image Restoration**

   * Removes blur caused by camera motion or poor focus.
   * Reduces noise using filters such as Wiener or inverse filtering.

3. **Noise Reduction**

   * Eliminates unwanted noise while preserving important image details.
   * Common filters: Median filter, Gaussian filter.

<summary>(d) i. Gamma Correction</summary>

Gamma correction is a nonlinear operation used to adjust the brightness of an image so that it appears natural on a display. Human vision perceives brightness nonlinearly, and monitors also have nonlinear intensity responses. Gamma correction compensates for this effect.

The transformation is:

$$
\boxed{s=c,r^{\gamma}}
$$

where:

* (r) = input pixel intensity (normalized to 0–1)
* (s) = output intensity
* (c) = constant (usually 1)
* ($\gamma$) = gamma value

**Effect of Gamma**

* **($\gamma < 1$):**

  * Brightens the image.
  * Enhances details in dark regions.

* **($\gamma > 1$):**

  * Darkens the image.
  * Preserves details in bright regions.

* **($\gamma = 1$):**

  * No change in brightness.

**Effect on Perceived Contrast**

* Improves visibility of details in shadows or highlights.
* Produces images that match human visual perception.
* Prevents images from appearing too dark or too washed out on different displays.

</details>

<summary>(c) ii. Factors for Choosing an Appropriate Gamma Setting</summary>

When selecting a gamma setting, consider the following:

1. Type of Content
  * **Photos:** Standard gamma (about **2.2**) provides natural colors and balanced contrast.
  * **Movies:** Gamma around **2.2–2.4** enhances cinematic appearance and shadow detail.
  * **Documents/Text:** Slightly lower gamma can make text appear brighter and easier to read.

2. Ambient Lighting
  * **Bright room:** Higher brightness or lower gamma may improve visibility.
  * **Dark room:** Standard or slightly higher gamma reduces eye strain and maintains contrast.

3. Monitor Calibration
  * Use the monitor's recommended gamma (typically **2.2** for most LCD/LED monitors) for accurate color reproduction.

4. Visual Comfort (Eye Health)
  * Avoid excessively high brightness or incorrect gamma, as these can cause eye fatigue.
  * Proper gamma improves readability, reduces glare, and provides a more comfortable viewing experience during long periods of use.

A gamma value of **approximately 2.2** is generally recommended because it offers a good balance between accurate colors, proper contrast, and comfortable viewing for most everyday applications.

Advantages

* Stronger sharpening.
* Preserves more image information.
* Better for satellite, medical, and aerial images.
